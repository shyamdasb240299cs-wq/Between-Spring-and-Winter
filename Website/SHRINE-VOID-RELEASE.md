# Memory sanctuary implementation

The 2D gate uses its registered image projection for a 1.7-second approach with inward petals. An aligned, feathered copy of the supplied film's decoded first frame becomes the scene during the approach. The poster remains until the first video frame is presented, then the film fills the viewport and fades completely to black before the lazy 3D handoff. See SHRINE-VIDEO-ENTRY.md for registration and playback details. Mobile portrait displays a rotation prompt and suspends animation/audio; landscape resumes the sequence.

The 3D scene preloads shared model buffers/textures and compiles main/reflection materials behind black. One deterministic awakening clock controls arrival → seeking → charging → blackout → crystal relighting → ordered lanterns → awake. Arrival holds pitch black, then reveals only water and a small crystal 18 metres away. Walking is entirely input driven. At 3.4 metres, while facing the crystal, P or the touch button commits the awakening. A 3.8-second charge expands white light from the projected crystal position. The black curtain covers this bloom before it is removed, preventing a brief glimpse of the crystal. Complete darkness lasts two seconds, then the crystal gradually relights over 2.2 seconds. Lanterns illuminate by distance, followed by trees, stars and petals over 5.4 seconds. Reduced motion removes crystal shake/white flash and shortens the reveal while preserving the two-second pause. Movement is blocked during the committed sequence and photo viewing, with input reset on focus loss, hidden tabs and dismissal.

The downloaded Jagobo original three trees retain authored near geometry and bark/blossom maps. Mid/far meshes, shared WebP textures, batched instances, bounded pools, GPU petal motion and low-detail reflection geometry limit rendering cost. Coverage-corrected blossom mipmaps retain flower silhouettes, and per-tree bounding-sphere culling skips offscreen geometry while reflections remain independently populated. The owner's Magic Crystal B&W GLB retains every actual mesh, including the moon and sparkles, and its original black-and-white materials. Prebuilt Khronos lanterns and individual Poly Haven scanned rocks replace procedural substitutes. Rock collections are separated before instancing; the old implementation repeated all six rocks for every placement.

Arrow Up/Down or W/S walk; Arrow Left/Right turn; A/D step sideways; dragging looks. Mobile uses the joystick and drag look. Photograph pillars face the entrance; the first memory is 28 metres away, beyond the crystal. Islands block passage through monuments once awake. Objects recycle as travel advances, with world-coordinate rebasing every 72 metres. Photos repeat in the infinite environment, using the existing five-image manifest.

Desktop rendering stays at native CSS resolution, with a 1.9-million-pixel ceiling for large screens; mobile can use up to 1.4× resolution. It never gradually shrinks to the old 58% setting. A single edge-selective output pass preserves edges and converts the display colour, eightfold texture anisotropy preserves oblique details, and the nearby Jagobo canopy always retains original geometry. Distant rocks/lanterns and all reflected secondary objects use offline-generated LODs with shared original PBR maps. The scene uses one world render and one combined edge/display pass, avoiding multisample overdraw and multiple full-screen blur passes; small local lamp halos replace expensive global bloom. Reflections use a dedicated lower-detail scene and 512/896-class targets with a throttled, world-registered projection. Nonblocking hardware timer queries distinguish actual GPU cost from slow browser scheduling; secondary quality adapts without destroying image resolution. Hidden tabs stop rendering. Exit disposes scene-owned resources and loses the old context; shared immutable asset buffers remain cached for re-entry. This is rasterized WebGL with bounded planar reflection geometry, not ray tracing. Reference-like composition is constrained by the actual Jagobo canopy geometry and integrated-GPU budgets; no guarantee of zero frame drops on every browser/device is made.

The scene background was regenerated at exactly 4× with the owner's installed Upscayl digital-art-4x model (887×1774 → 3548×7096). Lossless master: `Assets and working files/void-polish/background-crystal-entry-4x.png`. Responsive 960/1920/2880-pixel WebPs are served; the master is not loaded in the browser. The gate's original geometry/placement remains registered, with warmer matching, contact shading, a feathered base edge and opening light. Existing garden sprites, typography, reading and approved sounds are preserved.

Validation commands: `npm run lint`, `node scripts/test-void-navigation.mjs`, `node scripts/test-void-awakening.mjs`, `node scripts/test-shrine-video.mjs`, `node scripts/test-water-contacts.mjs`, TypeScript source checking (excluding stale framework-generated route declarations), and `npm run build:vercel`. Local Windows dependency tracing uses the existing ignored copy-serialization preload. Live UI checks cover short/long holds, crystal proximity, P activation, ordered reveal, joystick, held-key release, photo focus/keyboard handling, exit/re-entry and portrait blocking. Reduced-motion and pointer lifecycle contracts are covered by deterministic tests; physical mobile touch and reduced-motion browser emulation are not exposed by the current browser tool.

Asset attribution is visible from the sanctuary's Asset credits link. Original uncommitted scene files and tracked changes were saved in the working-files backup before rebuilding.

Reflection motion refinement: the planar target is captured every frame when the camera position, orientation or projection changes. Idle views retain the 18/30 Hz limit, using the existing lightweight reflection scene. This removes the stale camera viewpoint that made reflections drag during walking. Blossoms retain their original textures and alpha silhouettes with a modest emissive lift. Falling petals use an authored notched sakura silhouette with blush gradients and veins; a small, instanced local halo adds glow while preserving the sharp centre. There is no added full-screen bloom or blur pass. The story uses only its original painted background torii, with a soft opening light and no extra foreground gate.

The original black-and-white crystal sits exactly on a levelled scanned-rock support, with a low surrounding ring of rocks. Its model base, moon, stars and original material culling are unchanged. Water uses a nonuniform 128×128 grid, dense around the camera and sparse at the horizon. Centimetre wave heights and analytic surface normals change both geometry and reflected rays. Six damped contact wave fields modify that same water surface; the old flat ring decals and impacts projected 2.5 metres ahead are removed. Alternating foot contacts occur .36 metres ahead with .19-metre lateral spacing, with small lit 3D ballistic droplets. The first contact occurs after .14 metres of actual travel, followed by 1.65-metre stride intervals. Audio shares that contact event and uses an interactive low-latency context with predecoded, promptly trimmed real shallow-water footsteps. Movement integrates in bounded 60Hz physics steps so walking speed does not slow when rendering fluctuates. Subtle head motion and spray are disabled for reduced motion. Contacts and sounds follow actual travel, including joystick/sideways movement, and stop when stationary or blocked. The water is an analytic real-time surface approximation, not a fluid solver or ray-traced refraction.

The approved cinematic audio mix uses gentle airy entry, layered glass crystal tones, a two-second hush, crystal relighting and sequential lantern cues. After the owner's later request to proceed without further audio approval, the flowing ambience was replaced with Daen23's CC0 gentle-water recording. A three-second crossfade, rotated PCM boundary and padding-free WAV produce a seamless quiet still-water loop; it starts as the crystal reappears. The footsteps now use ChristopherJngs's CC0 recording of actual shallow-water foot contacts, isolated into three quiet .43-second cues. A shared on/off preference controls story and sanctuary sound, respects browser gesture requirements, and suppresses sounds in hidden tabs. Audio is decoded once and does not run per-frame DSP. Existing approved story music is retained and fades during entry. `node scripts/test-water-contacts.mjs` checks immediate contact, cadence at 30/60/120Hz, alternating feet, stationary/blocked silence and strafing.

During the sanctuary, the story stage stops painting and its CSS motion is paused. The stage stays mounted with its scroll space intact, so returning restores the existing story and sound controls. Offline asset tools are scripts/build-sanctuary-lods.py and scripts/build-blossom-mips.py.
