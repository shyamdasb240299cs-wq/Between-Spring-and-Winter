# Memory sanctuary implementation

The 2D gate still uses its registered image projection. Overlapping portal and spiral effects join through one entry clock, with inward petals throughout. The spiral is rendered within a 420,000-pixel budget, then fades completely to black and releases its WebGL context. It never reveals the home page during the lazy 3D handoff.

The 3D scene preloads shared model buffers/textures and compiles main/reflection materials behind black. One deterministic awakening clock controls arrival → seeking → charging → blackout → crystal relighting → ordered lanterns → awake. Arrival holds pitch black, then reveals only water and a small crystal 18 metres away. Walking is entirely input driven. At 3.4 metres, while facing the crystal, P or the touch button commits the awakening. A 3.8-second charge expands white light from the projected crystal position. The black curtain covers this bloom before it is removed, preventing a brief glimpse of the crystal. Complete darkness lasts two seconds, then the crystal gradually relights over 2.2 seconds. Lanterns illuminate by distance, followed by trees, stars and petals over 5.4 seconds. Reduced motion removes crystal shake/white flash and shortens the reveal while preserving the two-second pause. Movement is blocked during the committed sequence and photo viewing, with input reset on focus loss, hidden tabs and dismissal.

The downloaded Jagobo original three trees retain authored near geometry and bark/blossom maps. Mid/far meshes, shared WebP textures, batched instances, bounded pools, GPU petal motion, low-detail reflection geometry and quarter-size Gaussian glow limit rendering cost. The owner's Magic Crystal B&W GLB retains every actual mesh, including the moon and sparkles, and its original black-and-white materials. Prebuilt Khronos lanterns and individual Poly Haven scanned rocks replace procedural substitutes. Rock collections are separated before instancing; the old implementation repeated all six rocks for every placement.

Arrow Up/Down or W/S walk; Arrow Left/Right turn; A/D step sideways; dragging looks. Mobile uses the joystick and drag look. Photograph pillars face the entrance; the first memory is 28 metres away, beyond the crystal. Islands block passage through monuments once awake. Objects recycle as travel advances, with world-coordinate rebasing every 72 metres. Photos repeat in the infinite environment, using the existing five-image manifest.

Desktop render resolution is adaptive, capped at 1.05 million pixels, with no device-pixel-ratio multiplication. Reflections use a dedicated lower-detail scene and 384/512-class render targets; stationary reflection updates are throttled, moving views update promptly. Hidden tabs stop rendering. Exit disposes scene-owned resources and loses the old context; shared immutable asset buffers remain cached for re-entry. This is rasterized WebGL with bounded planar reflection geometry, not ray tracing. Reference-like composition is constrained by the actual Jagobo canopy geometry and real-time integrated-GPU budgets; no guarantee of zero frame drops on every browser/device is made.

The scene background was regenerated at exactly 4× with the owner's installed Upscayl digital-art-4x model (887×1774 → 3548×7096). Lossless master: `Assets and working files/void-polish/background-crystal-entry-4x.png`. Responsive 960/1920/2880-pixel WebPs are served; the master is not loaded in the browser. The gate's original geometry/placement remains registered, with warmer matching, contact shading, a feathered base edge and opening light. Existing garden sprites, typography, reading and approved sounds are preserved.

Validation commands: `npm run lint`, `node scripts/test-void-navigation.mjs`, `node scripts/test-void-awakening.mjs`, `node "../Assets and working files/shrine-entry/check-entry.mjs"`, TypeScript source checking (excluding stale framework-generated route declarations), and `npm run build:vercel`. Local Windows dependency tracing uses the existing ignored copy-serialization preload. Live UI checks cover short/long holds, crystal proximity, P activation, ordered reveal, joystick, held-key release, photo focus/keyboard handling, exit/re-entry and portrait layout. Reduced-motion and pointer lifecycle contracts are covered by deterministic tests; physical mobile touch and reduced-motion browser emulation are not exposed by the current browser tool.

Asset attribution is visible from the sanctuary's Asset credits link. Original uncommitted scene files and tracked changes were saved in the working-files backup before rebuilding.

The original black-and-white crystal sits exactly on a levelled scanned-rock support, with a low surrounding ring of rocks. Its model base, moon, stars and original material culling are unchanged. Water uses small interfering wavelets to perturb reflection UVs, without artificial blue lines. Twelve reusable footstep impacts provide instanced expanding rings and small ballistic droplets; subtle head motion is disabled for reduced motion. Impacts and quiet alternating wading sounds follow distance actually travelled, including joystick and sideways movement, and stop on blocked input.

The approved cinematic audio mix uses gentle airy entry, layered glass crystal tones, a two-second hush, crystal relighting and sequential lantern cues. After the owner's later request to proceed without further audio approval, the flowing ambience was replaced with Daen23's CC0 gentle-water recording. A three-second crossfade, rotated PCM boundary and padding-free WAV produce a seamless quiet still-water loop; it starts as the crystal reappears. Three short wading clips come from the same recording. A shared on/off preference controls story and sanctuary sound, respects browser gesture requirements, and suppresses sounds in hidden tabs. Audio is decoded once and does not run per-frame DSP. Existing approved story music is retained and fades during entry.
